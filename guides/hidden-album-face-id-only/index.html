Can the iPhone Hidden album be Face ID only?

No. The Hidden album opens with Face ID or with your iPhone passcode, and iOS has no setting that turns the passcode off. It also cannot have a passcode of its own.

Make the Hidden album ask for Face ID

  • Open Settings, then Apps, then Photos.
  • Turn on Use Face ID. It applies to the Hidden and Recently Deleted albums.

With that on, opening the album asks for Face ID. If Face ID fails, or someone knows your passcode, the passcode opens it too.

Who else can open it

Anyone who knows the iPhone passcode. The hidden video also stays in your library: if iCloud Photos is on, it still syncs to every device on your Apple Account, and it still counts toward your storage.

A separate locked place for videos

Pillowfeed is an app that keeps videos apart from Photos. It checks your identity with Face ID and falls back to the iPhone passcode the same way the Hidden album does, so it is not Face ID only either. What differs is where the videos live: inside the app, not in your camera roll.

  • It locks again after 5 minutes away from the app, and the app switcher shows a cover instead of your videos.
  • Import from Photos or Files, then delete the originals from Photos and empty Recently Deleted if you want them out of the library.
  • No account, and the developer runs no servers.

If you need a lock that someone who knows your passcode cannot open, neither the Hidden album nor Pillowfeed is it.

Common questions

Can the iPhone Hidden album be Face ID only?

No. The Hidden album opens with Face ID, Touch ID or your iPhone passcode. iOS has no setting that removes the passcode fallback.

How do I make the Hidden album ask for Face ID?

Open Settings, then Apps, then Photos, and turn on Use Face ID. This covers the Hidden and Recently Deleted albums.

Can the Hidden album have a different passcode?

No. It uses the passcode of the iPhone, so anyone who knows that passcode can open it.
