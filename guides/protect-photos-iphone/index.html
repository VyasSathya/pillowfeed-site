How to protect your photos on iPhone: five steps

Most of the protection comes from your passcode, because every lock on the iPhone falls back to it. After that, five settings matter.

1. A passcode only you know

Use a long passcode. Anyone who knows it can open a locked app, such as Photos.

2. Lock the Photos app

Touch and hold the Photos icon and tap Require Face ID.

3. Hide sensitive items

Touch and hold the photo and tap Hide. On iOS 16 and later the Hidden album is locked by default.

4. Remove location when you share

In the Share sheet, tap Options and turn off Location, so a shared photo does not carry where it was taken.

5. Think about iCloud

iCloud Photos copies your library to Apple's servers. Advanced Data Protection, in Settings under your name, then iCloud, adds end-to-end encryption for photos. It means you must keep a recovery method yourself.

Common questions

How do I make sure my photos are private on iPhone?

Use a strong passcode, require Face ID on the Photos app, put sensitive items in the Hidden album, remove location when you share, and consider Advanced Data Protection for iCloud.

Is there a secret place to store photos on iPhone?

The Hidden album under Utilities in Photos is the built-in place. It is locked by default on iOS 16 and later but stays in your library.

Can I put a lock on photos on my iPhone?

Yes. Require Face ID locks the Photos app, and the Hidden album has its own lock.
